Recent behavioral studies in animal cognition demonstrate that New Caledonian crows exhibit sophisticated problem-solving abilities, including bending twigs into hooks to extract grubs from tree crevices. This tool-manufacturing capacity is not merely instinctual; young crows observe experienced adults and incrementally refine their crafting technique, indicating cultural transmission of tool-use behavior.

OPTIONS:
A) New Caledonian crows fabricate hooked tools through learning and adult observation, demonstrating cultural transmission of problem-solving skills rather than pure instinct.
B) Crows possess innate genetic instructions that dictate the exact angle of hooked twigs without requiring any social observation or cognitive processing.
C) Tool use among avian species has been entirely debunked as human researcher bias, since birds lack hands and prehensile digits.
